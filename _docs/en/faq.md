Gitpay connects useful work with payment. Start with [What is Gitpay?](/docs/en/getting-started/) for the two main paths: project contributions and Payment Requests for delivered work.

## Do I need to find a bounty first?
No. A funded issue provides an agreed scope and amount, but a contributor can also start through normal project collaboration. Ask the maintainer whether and how the contribution will be paid before assuming an accepted change earns a payment. See [Work on an issue](/docs/en/contributor/).

## What is the difference between a bounty and a Payment Request?
A bounty starts with a project funding an issue. A Payment Request starts with someone asking a customer to pay for delivered work. Both can result in a transfer and payout, but they have different approval paths. See [Fund an issue](/docs/en/sponsor/) and [Charge customers](/docs/en/service-provider/).

## Can a client pay without GitHub?
Yes. A customer can open the provider's Payment Request link, review the request, and pay through checkout. The provider and client should agree on scope and amount before the link is sent.

## When does the provider receive money?
Payment, transfer credit, and payout are separate stages. Processing and payout setup can affect timing. Read [Payments](/docs/en/payments/), [Claims](/docs/en/claims/), and [Payouts](/docs/en/payouts/).

## Can teams and organizations use Gitpay?
Yes. A team can fund project issues or pay a professional's Payment Request. Agree internally who controls funding, who accepts delivery, and which account receives the funds. These are coordination steps; the docs do not assume a dedicated organization billing feature.

## What if work or payment is disputed?
Keep the issue, Pull Request, delivery, and approval records. Read [Disputes and refunds](/docs/en/disputes-and-refunds/) for the documented payment effects and contact support for a specific transaction.
