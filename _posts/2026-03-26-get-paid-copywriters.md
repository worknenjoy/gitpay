## The problem: the words are done, the money isn't

Copy is a strange deliverable to invoice for. It's not a physical object, it's rarely "final" in the
client's mind until it's live on the page, and it's easy for a client to sit on a finished draft for
a week without any real work left to do on their side. Meanwhile you're waiting on an invoice that's
somewhere in a queue.

A **payment request** — a link the client can pay right away — shortens that gap by turning payment
into a single action instead of a document someone has to process.

## Where the delay usually comes from

- The draft is delivered, but "final" review drags on for reasons unrelated to the writing itself.
- Invoices go to someone who wasn't part of the project and doesn't recognize the request.
- Smaller copy jobs (a single landing page, one email) don't justify a full invoicing setup for either side.
- International clients add bank-transfer delays and fees on top of the wait.

## A workflow that keeps payment attached to delivery

1. Finish and deliver the copy (doc, Google Doc comment-ready link, or CMS draft).
2. In the same message, include a short note on what's included and the payment request link.
3. The client pays, or forwards internally for approval.

Sending the request in a separate follow-up message is the single most common reason payment stalls
— it turns a bundled action into two things the client can put off.

## Example scenarios

**Landing page copy.** Deliverable: final copy for a page, ready to hand to a designer or drop into a
CMS. Attach the payment request the moment the copy is marked final — not after it goes live, which
depends on someone else's timeline.

**Email sequence.** Deliverable: a set of emails (welcome series, launch sequence, nurture flow).
Bill against the agreed sequence length once all emails are delivered, rather than waiting to see
open or click rates — performance isn't something you control after handoff.

**Blog content package.** Deliverable: an agreed batch of articles or posts. For ongoing content
work, billing per batch (rather than per article) keeps the payment request from becoming a constant
interruption.

**Product messaging / positioning.** Deliverable: a positioning doc, key messages, or a style guide.
This kind of strategic writing is easy to undersell — treat the finished document as the deliverable,
distinct from whatever marketing execution comes after it.

## What to include with the request

Attach a short summary next to the link so the client doesn't hesitate over what exactly they're
paying for:

- What was delivered (page copy, sequence, batch, positioning doc)
- Where to review it (doc link, CMS draft)
- The payment request link

## If the client needs a formal invoice too

A payment request doesn't replace a client's accounting requirements — some finance teams still need
a formal invoice on file. That's fine: use the request as the "pay now" action, and provide the
invoice alongside it if asked. This isn't tax advice; check your own obligations if you're unsure.
